Reveille turns your phone into the power button for your computer.

Named after the bugle call that wakes a barracks at dawn, it does one thing and
tries to do it properly: start, shut down, restart, sleep and lock your Windows,
macOS or Linux machine over your own Wi-Fi.

<b>Setting it up</b>

One line pasted into a terminal on the computer. It installs what it needs, sets
itself to start when you log in, and shows a QR code for the phone to scan. No
account, no sign-up, and no administrator rights for the normal install.

<b>What it can do</b>

* Wake a computer that is fully switched off, using Wake-on-LAN
* Shut down, restart, sleep and lock
* Shut down or restart on a timer, and cancel it again
* Reboot straight into the BIOS or firmware screen (Windows only, and only if
  you say yes when it asks)
* Show what the machine is doing: CPU, memory and disk
* Tell you when the machine is still at its sign-in screen, rather than
  pretending it is offline

<b>It stays on your network</b>

There is no cloud service, no account and no telemetry. Your phone talks
straight to your computer over your own Wi-Fi, and nothing is sent anywhere
else. Requests from outside your local network are refused outright.

The pairing secret never crosses the network either: each request is signed with
it, so anyone watching the traffic learns nothing they could reuse. Signatures
cannot be replayed, and the computer signs its replies so your phone can tell it
apart from anything else answering.
